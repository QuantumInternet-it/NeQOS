Hardware safety
===============

Software validation checks declared contracts, topology, readiness and resource
ownership. It does not measure optical power, verify laboratory wiring or
replace the equipment manufacturer's operating limits and safety procedures.
The operator's Start action can operate real devices.

Before starting or activating
-----------------------------

1. Verify device identities, serial numbers, endpoints, optical paths and
   detector/readout pairing against the actual equipment.
2. Review source operating modes, power and attenuation requests. An example
   value, particularly zero attenuation, is not a safe value for your testbed.
3. Verify each detector's permitted illumination and bias against its
   manufacturer's limits and the laboratory's procedures.
4. Inspect the activation changes table, persistent calibration options and
   the protocol's restoration policy before accepting changes.
5. Confirm that no other process or computer owns the same hardware.
   NeQOS endpoint locks and leases are not a laboratory-wide physical interlock.

No universal power or bias limit is prescribed here: it depends on the exact
source, detector, optical losses and hardware configuration.

Detector health during acquisition
-----------------------------------

A latch guard uses declared detector channels paired to the acquisition's
TimeTagger channels. An available **G** marker means a guard can be resolved,
not that the current run enabled it. Read the protocol's explicit
``latchGuard`` policy.

For guarded finite acquisitions, a latch-invalid window is discarded.
A retry requires successful recovery evidence, valid health and remaining
retry budget. Monitoring instead annotates invalid windows without retrying
them. Count-rate traces, zero counts and a successful terminal state alone
do not prove detector safety or measurement validity.
See :doc:`error-reference` for the report classifications.

Abort, cleanup and recovery
---------------------------

``Abort run`` stops the submitted workflow and invokes its declared cleanup.
It is not a hardware emergency-stop guarantee and not a whole-experiment undo.
Persistent changes and completed prior steps can remain. Follow the
laboratory's emergency procedures when physical equipment requires intervention.

Keep the relevant processes running until cleanup finishes, then inspect
restoration replies. Missing cleanup evidence is unknown, not successful
restoration. After failed activation, reconcile configuration drift before
running another scientific protocol.

Use :doc:`quickstart` for software-only checks before connecting hardware.
